It has been a month since I last gave an update on my book, and I have made a few steps in
developing it. In the past month, I logged six hours of work and developed a rough frame for Section
II. In this blog post, I want to first reflect on my writing process, share the current state of the
book’s structure, and then discuss the next steps I will be taking.

When I first started preparing the second section, I spent about two hours researching a set of
questions based on areas I want to explore in the section. This research was quite broad, and I felt
that it reduced the effectiveness of my work and the directed nature of my research. The research
primarily focused on the emotional effects of screen usage, as well as broader trends in how workers
are using artificial intelligence and technology. In regard to this aspect of the preparation
process, I feel that I need to identify narrower questions and trim a majority of the ones that I
first settled upon. I thus identified two courses of action I will take for this aspect of the
preparation process.

1. Use books over research papers and articles to build a base of understanding in the area.
2. Narrow down areas of research in Part II and identify those key areas of Section I where I need
   to reinforce certain ideas to back up crucial points.

The second major event that occurred in regard to my writing process in this past month was taking a
set of classes at MIT as part of a program called Splash. The day I had on campus was quite
intensive, but it opened my mind up to numerous ideas about how to expand the book and skewed its
purpose toward artificial intelligence. Of the six classes I took, the two I felt I could connect
most with this book were Steven Faerm’s lecture on how to use artificial intelligence in fashion
design and Christian Ferko’s lecture on artificial intelligence. These lectures and the ideas I
gained from them are going to be incorporated into the third section, which I will begin to draft
once I have written the second section verbatim.

Now that I have shared the course of my writing process over the past month, I want to share the
structure of Part II. Part II is divided into two chapters. The first focuses on understanding
technology to apply that knowledge to make better decisions, and the latter focuses on the major
decisions students will likely make in regard to technology.

Originally, I planned to keep a unifying chapter on intentionality, which would explain how using
technology in an effective and responsible manner would honor God, but I decided against it. I plan
to incorporate the remnants of the intended chapter in the introduction and conclusion of Part II.

My first chapter is divided into three movements. The first focuses on the physical effects of
technology usage on the brain. I want the audience to specifically understand three questions which
I believe are most connected to students in this day and age. First, how does the brain deal with
excessive stimulation of any kind? And how does stimulation affect day-to-day well-being, learning,
and development of the mind? Second, how does the brain function in long, uninterrupted work
sessions compared to short bursts? Third, I’ll cover shorter, less in-depth questions about topics
such as the effects of handwriting versus digital typing and blue light exposure. I believe these
questions will give the reader an excellent understanding of how the brain interacts with
technology.

The second movement focuses on how tools such as artificial intelligence function.

I believe that explaining how artificial intelligence functions is important in a book that focuses
on digital habits because once a student understands the technology, they will be able to use the
tool more effectively. I have divided this second movement into two sections. The first focuses on
developing a very simple mathematical intuition for artificial intelligence and neural networks,
which I have developed from CS231n lectures, 3Blue1Brown, and past experiences reading and listening
to these topics. I believe I will be able to communicate the concepts intuitively and through
metaphors to an audience with an algebra-level understanding of mathematics. I myself have developed
two unique analogies for the mathematics behind the models: the burning of Rome from Quo Vadis and
my father trying to fix his car. My goal is to stress the importance of mathematics and its role in
these models without going into detail about how mathematics and physics tie into them. I hope that
if the audience understands that a model is simply a statistical machine, they will develop a unique
view of how to use it, which might help them. The latter of these sections will focus on
understanding how AI capabilities are specifically tied to computation and data. I will explain that
applying the mathematical concepts I mentioned requires immense calculation capabilities. I will
also explain the scaling laws I first learned about in a podcast in which Jensen Huang discussed
challenges to scaling. I find it particularly helpful that two years ago I wrote a research paper on
Moore’s Law. I am looking forward to applying this information in my book.

The next movement focuses on the historical events that have led to the modern technology landscape.
I’ve identified three lines to follow. The first is the industry, the second is capabilities, and
the third is the application field. In each of these areas, I will cover a few key events that
connect with developments in the other two categories. In this movement, I will also stress the
importance of developing the ability to make long-term predictions that connect with the personal
decisions we make with digital tools on a day-to-day basis.

Continuing on to the second chapter of Part II, I use the information that I have communicated in
previous chapters to address what I believe are common decisions students will make about how they
use digital tools. This chapter has three main movements. The first is AI’s role in the lives of
students, the second is finding good resources on the web, and the third asks how big my digital
toolkit should be. In Part I, I urged students to make personal agreements that are honest to
themselves and who the Father made them to be. In the same way, I argue that students should make
their own decisions when faced with these questions. Nevertheless, I have developed my own opinions
on artificial intelligence usage in education, which I will voice in this chapter. Alongside my
personal opinions, I will share my arguments for both sides of the debate about artificial
intelligence usage, as well as the best course of action for those who decide to explore this new
technology.

This chapter concludes my current structure for Part II, which I have been working on for the past
few months. In the coming weeks and vacations, I want to work on two areas I’ve identified as
challenges while preparing this section. The most pressing is simply the amount of time spent on the
book. I’ve consistently lagged behind my desired goal of four hours per week. I believe I have
averaged about one hour and thirty minutes a week, spent in a single, moderate-quality working
session. Sundays and the weekdays were simply absent from my writing process, and most of my work
sessions were Saturday mornings. If I want to make steady progress toward my December 31 deadline, I
must resolve to spend at least three hours per week working on the project. My goal during Christmas
and Thanksgiving break is to spend a moderate three to four hours a day on the book and do it
consistently.

The second element I will commence following this blog post is the verbatim write-up of Part II.
Looking back on the previous section I wrote word for word, I plan to spend much more time in this
period of the writing process to create a level of clarity that cannot be met by simply jotting down
my thoughts. Once that section has been completed, I plan to return to the introduction and
conclusion of Part I and attempt to merge them seamlessly. At that point, when the gap has been
bridged, I’ll either return to refining and trimming Parts I and II or begin focusing my efforts on
the final section of the book. Third, as I mentioned earlier, I will also devote time to reading
books related to topics that I have been exploring. Over Christmas and the coming months, I hope to
observe how other leaders in this field have communicated their opinions on digital habits.

Apart from these works, I also want to write a brief testimony of faith following my baptism and
share my specific views on guilt and condemnation, the warning of falling away from Hebrews, and how
